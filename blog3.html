Food Waste and Organic Waste Problems

Food Waste and Organic Waste Problems

Large amounts of food and organic waste are thrown away daily from homes, restaurants, and markets. Instead of being reused or composted, this waste is often mixed with general garbage, which makes disposal more difficult and less efficient.

Organic waste decomposes quickly and produces unpleasant smells and harmful gases when not properly managed, contributing to air pollution and attracting pests in surrounding areas.

The Organic Waste

What are the Effects?

Enviromental Effects

Long-term damage to natural habitats and biodiversity loss

Release of methane gas from decomposing food waste, which contributes to global warming and significantly accelerates climate change when organic waste is left unmanaged in landfills.

Increased landfill size due to unnecessary organic waste accumulation, which places extra pressure on already limited waste disposal sites and reduces available land over time.

Pollution of soil and water when food waste is not properly managed, causing harmful substances to seep into the environment and contaminate natural resources used by people and wildlife.

In Myanmar, unmanaged waste attracts pests such as flies, rats, and other rodents, which can disturb local ecosystems, create unpleasant living conditions, and increase the risk of disease in nearby communities.

Health Effects

Long-term damage to natural habitats and biodiversity loss

Growth of bacteria and harmful microorganisms in decaying food waste, creating unhealthy conditions that can negatively affect both people and the environment.

Spread of diseases through contaminated food and water sources, especially in areas where waste is not properly collected or treated.

Waste accumulation and poorly managed landfill sites often produce unpleasant odors that affect community living conditions and reduce the overall quality of life for nearby residents.

Increased risk of infections in areas with poor waste management, particularly where waste accumulates near homes, markets, and public spaces.
