Waste Management in Myanmar

Myanmar’s waste management system mainly relies on:

  • Open dumping
  • Landfills with limited treatment
  • Minimal recycling systems
    •

In cities like Yangon, thousands of tons of waste are generated daily, yet only a portion is properly collected and managed. Many areas still lack consistent waste collection services, leading to waste being burned or left in open spaces, which causes environmental and health problems.

There is also limited infrastructure for waste segregation at source, so recyclable materials are often mixed with general waste, reducing recycling efficiency.

Why is Waste a Problem
in Myanmar?

  • Poor waste collection systems – Many areas don’t have regular pickup, so trash piles up in streets and open spaces.
  • Limited recycling facilities – Most materials end up in landfills or rivers instead of being reused.
  • Low public awareness – Communities are still learning about proper disposal, so littering remains common.
  • Plastic dependency – Single‑use plastics dominate daily life and create long‑lasting pollution.
  • Illegal dumping – Waste is often thrown into rivers or streets, blocking drains and worsening floods.
  • The Impact - Together, these problems cause pollution, flooding, and serious health risks for communities across the country.

How Myanmar Currently Manages Waste

  • Waste is collected by municipal authorities, mainly in urban areas such as Yangon and Mandalay.
  • Most collected waste is disposed of in landfill sites, which are often overcrowded and poorly managed.
  • Informal recycling is commonly carried out by waste pickers who collect plastic, metal, paper, and other reusable materials for income.

Although efforts are being made in Myanmar to improve waste management, more sustainable, efficient, and community-driven solutions are still needed to reduce pollution and protect public health.
